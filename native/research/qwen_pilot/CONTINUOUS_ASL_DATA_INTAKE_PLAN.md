# Continuous ASL data intake plan

**November 19, 2026 · Research plan.** The product goal is everyday ASL conversation on the phone. The app is free and Apache-2.0, with entirely local inference and no collection of customer frames, audio, landmarks, text, corrections or other conversation data. Developer research uses separately sourced, permitted published videos and expert references. Prefer project-authored tools, rubrics and engineering fixtures and freely licensed material. No recruitment of Deaf participants is planned.

The previous pair trained on 16 size-selected translated general-text clips and scored 0/8 exact on the fresh FSboard fingerspelling examples. The frozen FSboard v4 whole-prompt proposal uses 8 to 32 distinct ordered frames; full-media and processor qualification remain pending. Continuous sentence and discourse translation need evidence from signed utterances with reliable meaning references. This plan advances that work. It admits no new dataset and qualifies no training or product claim. ## 2. Reserve independent evaluation and verify a media sentinel

1. **Assign groups before selecting rows.** Group by canonical FLORES ID where comparable, normalized prompt and near-duplicate text, source document, recording/session, publisher-supplied signer group, file and clip digest, and derived cuts or augmentations. Combine linked groups across sources and compare with previously viewed or used examples. Resolve duplicate translations, overlapping intervals and shared publications before assigning roles. Log unresolved identity scope and unknown base pretraining exposure. Use publisher identifiers and provenance, with no facial matching or identity inference.
2. **Freeze disjoint roles.** Version and hash train, development, calibration and sealed final-evaluation manifests and scoring rules before tuning. Keep previously inspected clips out of the final set. Reserve FLEURS-ASL document/recording and prompt groups first. Remove linked Meta examples from training and tuning. Reserve a separate everyday ASL source with enough known independent signers and recordings. If group evidence is insufficient, mark that slice as limited and add an independent source before making a signer-general or conversation claim. Keep sealed references and labels out of training prompts, filenames exposed to the model and checkpoint selection.
3. **Start small after rights and access review.** Proposed local intake cap is up to four sentence windows per first-priority source and one FLEURS-ASL discourse recording, within 256 MiB total fetched and 512 MiB scratch, including container overhead. Select sentinel records from a development-only reserve. Check bytes and expected fetch size before requesting media. Stop if the supported access route requires a larger download. Record actual sizes and SHA-256 hashes for original media, reference files, prepared inputs and manifest. Record source/recording and signer-group IDs, reference version and speaker/turn, exact start/end timestamps and time base, crop and transformation history, and actual sampled frame times.
4. **Verify what the model sees and what the reference means.** Check decoding, display rotation, presentation timestamps, cadence, sampling and repeated or missing frames. Inspect whether both hands when used, face and upper body are visible at useful resolution over the segment. Preserve linguistic nonmanuals, such as eyebrow, head and body cues, through crop and sampling. No emotion inference or identity features. Cross-check each timed segment against a human-written published expert translation or interpretation. Record provenance, omissions, prior context, annotation disagreements and acceptable paraphrases. Treat prompt, gloss, voiceover, subtitles and captions as separate evidence fields. Keep ambiguous or unaligned examples out of scored meaning evidence and record the gap.

The intake receipt should show admitted bytes, counts and coverage by role, source, signer-group evidence and scenario, exclusions, failed media checks and manifest digests. A passing sentinel supports bounded intake expansion on the same pinned route.

## 3. Test sequence grounding and static transfer

- Author a coverage matrix for everyday turns such as introductions, requests, directions, timing, clarification and turn-taking. Look for permitted signing with questions, negation, reference to people and locations, names, numbers, fingerspelling within utterances, different signing rates and realistic camera variation. Add silence, non-sign motion, clipped turns, occlusion and out-of-scope cases for rejection. Use project-created synthetic cases for engineering controls and published expert references for linguistic claims.
- Use the owner-selected small Qwen and train our own adapters. Benchmark the frozen base and compare two arms on verified video from a common initialization. A uses video only. B adds the approved low-weight static handshape objective through its own sampler. Match video examples, ordered exposure, preprocessing, video loss, optimizer updates and decay, seed, and decoding. Report added static time and compute. Prespecify the static weight, trainable parameters, checkpoint rule and early stop. Ordered RGB stills need a pinned sampling contract. A native-video experiment needs its own timestamp, temporal-pairing and export/runtime parity checks in both arms.
- Compare matched clips against same-clip reversed and repeated-frame controls, matched-length black input and unrelated signing. Inspect input sensitivity together with meaning accuracy and critical errors. Score usable accepted meaning against the published references with a rubric for intent, negation, referents and names/numbers. Track unsupported content, critical substitutions, deferral rate, accepted precision, coverage and errors by scenario and source with grouped uncertainty. Keep exact match and character error rate for fingerspelling diagnostics. Select confidence and deferral cutoffs on calibration examples. Keep B only if the predeclared real-video transfer and continuous-meaning non-regression criteria pass.
- Flag insufficient or ambiguous input, offer a retry or correction, and prevent unsupported output from reaching speech. Require explicit review before speech. Freeze the product cutoffs and denominators before final scoring. The existing roadmap proposes at least 95% meaning-correct among accepted in-domain utterances, at least 80% coverage, and zero unsupported critical substitutions on the fixed critical suite. These are proposals. The exact offline candidate must also meet agreed sign-end-to-draft, sustained latency and device resource gates on both target phones. Set missing timings from a representative early baseline before final scoring and report tail latency, thermal behavior and actual scope coverage.
